Read Later — Privacy Policy

Last updated August 2026

In short: Read Later doesn't have a server. Everything you save stays in your own browser (or syncs between your own devices through your Google account, via Chrome's built-in sync — not anything this extension operates). Nothing is sold, shared, or sent anywhere for analytics or advertising, because nothing is sent anywhere at all.

What data is stored

When you save a page, Read Later stores:

  • The page's URL, title, and favicon image URL
  • When you saved it, and whether you've marked it read
  • The order you've arranged your list in
  • If you saved it using the on-page floating button specifically: a cached, readable copy of the article's text and images, extracted at save time (used for the offline-friendly reader view)

Read Later also stores a couple of your preferences (for example, whether the floating button is turned on) so they persist between browsing sessions.

Where it's stored

All of this is stored using Chrome's own built-in storage APIs, directly in your browser: chrome.storage.sync for your reading list and settings, and chrome.storage.local for cached article content (which is larger, and deliberately kept local rather than synced).

If you're signed into Chrome and have sync turned on, chrome.storage.sync data follows you to your other devices the same way your bookmarks or browsing history would — that syncing is handled entirely by Google/Chrome's own infrastructure, not by any server this extension runs, because this extension doesn't run a server.

What Read Later does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, or usage tracking of any kind
  • No advertising, and no data is ever sold or shared with third parties — there's no third party in this picture to share it with
  • No account or sign-up required to use the extension
  • No remote servers operated by this extension — the source code has no analytics SDK, no tracking pixel, and no network request to anywhere except what's described below

The only network requests it makes

When you view your saved list, your browser loads each article's favicon image directly from the site that hosts it — an ordinary image request, exactly like the one your browser already makes when you visit that site normally. That's the only outbound network activity this extension causes on its own.

Deleting your data

Since everything lives in your own browser, you're always in full control: delete individual saved articles from the Reading List Manager, or remove the extension entirely via chrome://extensions to delete everything at once. There's no account to close and no request to send anyone, because there's nothing stored anywhere but your own browser.

Changes to this policy

Read Later is open source. If this policy changes, the update will be in the same commit history as everything else — see the source and full history at github.com/karanchanglani/reading-list-extension. Questions or concerns can be raised as a GitHub issue there.
